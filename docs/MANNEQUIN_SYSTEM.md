# Mannequin engine — Phase 1 & 2

The mannequin/body system was rebuilt from scratch. This document covers what's
built, why it's built this way, and what's intentionally deferred. See
[`docs/MULTI_TENANCY.md`](./MULTI_TENANCY.md) for the (unrelated) tenancy work.

## Why

The previous mannequin (`src/components/3d/procedural/humanoid.ts` +
`src/components/3d/scene/MannequinFigure.tsx`) was a stack of raw Three.js
primitives — three tapered cylinders for the torso, a sphere for the head, two
capsules for arms, two plain cylinders for legs with no hands or feet. It read
as blocky/primitive rather than a fashion mannequin. This was a full rebuild
of the visible body, not a patch.

## Asset sourcing

The brief this was built against explicitly forbids shipping a fake primitive
mannequin and calling it finished, but also says not to fake a *production*
asset — if no real GLB is available, build the full engine architecture with a
placeholder that's trivial to swap later. Before writing any geometry code, a
bounded web search was done (Sketchfab, TurboSquid, CGTrader, itch.io,
Open3dModel, Meshy) for a free/CC0 mannequin mesh to use as that placeholder:

- A Sketchfab "Mannequin (CC0)" model (WuYin) — faceless, neutral body, but
  only 5.3k tris/2.7k verts, and its actual license field reads "CC
  Attribution (CC BY 4.0)" despite the title's "(CC0)" claim. No rig, no size
  variants, no gender split.
- An itch.io "[RIGGED] Low-Poly Mannequin" (b0gdan) — has a skeleton, but is
  unisex, described as "geometric low-poly" (risk of looking blocky again),
  and states no explicit commercial-use license terms.

Neither cleared *(commercial-safe license) AND (usable rig) AND (male+female)
AND (non-blocky quality) AND (GLB-ready)* simultaneously, and there's no
Blender/Maya available in this environment to retopologize or rig a found mesh
even if one were licensable. So the visible mesh is procedural — built by a
real geometric lofting technique, not primitive stacking — inside a genuinely
GLB-ready engine (real `SkinnedMesh`, real `Skeleton`, a real `useGLTF` loader
branch). Pointing a `Mannequin.baseModelUrl` at a licensed GLB later is a data
change, not a rebuild — see "Swapping in a real GLB" below.

## How the geometry is built

`src/components/3d/mannequin/geometry/ring-loft.ts` — `buildRingLoft()` is the
one building block every body part is made from. It lofts a smooth tube
through a chain of cross-section landmarks: each landmark gives a Y position,
a half-width (left-right) and *separate* front/back depths (this is what
makes a torso read as a torso instead of a cylinder — a real chest/belly
bulges forward more than the back curves backward), plus a superellipse
exponent controlling how squared vs. rounded that ring is. Landmark values are
Catmull-Rom-interpolated between control points, and every generated ring uses
the same radial segment count, which is what keeps the topology clean
ring-to-ring and is the precondition Phase 2's morph targets will need.

`src/components/3d/mannequin/geometry/body-chains.ts` — per-gender proportion
tables (`MALE_PROPORTIONS` / `FEMALE_PROPORTIONS`) and the chain builders
(`buildTorsoChain`, `buildArmChain`, `buildLegChain`, `buildFootChain`,
`buildFingerChain`). The two genders are independently tuned ratio tables
(waist-to-hip ratio, bust presence, shoulder-to-hip ratio, neck thickness),
not one mesh scaled for the other.

`src/components/3d/mannequin/MannequinBuilder.ts` — assembles one torso+neck+
head chain, two arm chains (each including its own palm), ten finger chains,
two leg chains, and two foot chains into a single `BufferGeometry`, and
computes skin weights per part along the way (see "Skinning" below).

### Parts meet by overlap, not exact vertex welding

A fully manifold mesh where the torso forks into two legs, and each palm
forks into five fingers, needs real branching topology — the kind normally
built by hand in a DCC tool with edge-loop control, not generated by a
straight-axis lofter. That's out of scope here (no Blender/Maya available).
Instead, each limb/hand/foot is its own smooth loft, positioned with a couple
of centimeters embedded into the parent part's volume (e.g. the arm's
`shoulderEmbed` landmark sits inside the torso, past where the arm socket
actually is). This reads as one coherent figure at normal viewing distance —
which is the literal complaint the old mannequin drew ("disconnected
primitives" meant visible gaps and mismatched primitive-to-primitive seams,
not "not a single watertight manifold") — without the risk of attempting
branching topology from scratch. Revisit if/when a real GLB replaces the
procedural mesh.

### Placement math

Every part is authored in its own local space (Y ascending along the part's
natural axis) and then rigidly placed with `Quaternion.setFromUnitVectors`
aligning local +Y to whatever world direction the part should hang/extend
toward, followed by a translation. This is always a proper rotation (never a
reflection), so normals and winding stay correct — see `buildPart()` /
`localToWorld()` in `MannequinBuilder.ts`.

## Skinning

Each part's vertices get two-bone skin weights assigned along that part's own
local Y axis (before placement), blended with a `smoothstep` between the two
nearest bone breakpoints. This is a documented first-pass heuristic — not a
proper heat-map/geodesic weight solve — explicitly allowed by the brief this
was built against ("fingers don't need realistic anatomical detail"). If
joints deform oddly once animation lands in a later phase, this is the first
place to revisit.

`src/components/3d/mannequin/MannequinSkeleton.ts` builds the requested bone
hierarchy (`Root → Pelvis → Spine → Spine_01 → Spine_02 → Chest → Neck →
Head`, `L/R_Shoulder → UpperArm → Forearm → Hand`, `L/R_UpperLeg → LowerLeg →
Foot → Toe`) directly from the world positions computed alongside the mesh, so
joints always land inside the geometry regardless of measurements. Bones are
left at identity rotation in the bind pose — nothing depends on a particular
bone-forward convention, since `SkinnedMesh.bind()` only cares about world
position at bind time.

### Why there's no literal T-pose anywhere

The brief asks for a non-T-pose default stance, with T-pose reserved for
rigging. Since there are no imported/retargeted animation clips yet (that's
Phase 3), there's no reason to bind in a literal horizontal-arms T-pose at
all: the base geometry is authored with arms already hanging at a natural
angle, and `applyPresentationPose()` adds a small fixed head tilt and
turned-out feet on top of that bind pose. Revisit this decision if Phase 3
imports an external animation clip that assumes a T-pose rest pose to
retarget against.

## Size morphing (Phase 2)

`src/components/3d/mannequin/MannequinMorphs.ts` — `buildMorphTargets()`
produces all 10 requested morph axes (height, shoulderWidth, chest, bust,
waist, hip, thigh, armVolume, legLength, torsoLength) by **re-running the
entire builder once per axis**, perturbing one parameter at a time off the
base measurements, and storing the resulting position delta. This only works
because the loft's topology (ring/vertex count and order) is a pure function
of the profile definition, never of the measurement values — confirmed by a
hard vertex-count-mismatch check in `buildMorphTargets()` itself, and by the
Vitest suite. Two kinds of axis exist:

- **Measurement-backed axes** (height, shoulderWidth, chest, waist, hip,
  thigh, legLength) perturb one `MannequinMeasurements` field directly. The
  same delta is reused to *solve* size → weight
  (`resolveSizeMorphWeights()`): `weight = (targetSize[field] -
  reference[field]) / delta`.
- **Proportion-backed axes** (bust, armVolume, torsoLength) have no single
  circumference to perturb, so they go through
  `MannequinBuilder.ts`'s `MannequinMorphOverrides` instead — bust perturbs
  the chest landmark's `frontBulge` (prominence independent of chest
  circumference), armVolume perturbs `GenderProportions.armVolume` (bicep/
  forearm thickness), torsoLength perturbs a `torsoLengthScale` factor that
  stretches only the pelvis-to-shoulder span in `buildTorsoChain()` (neck/head
  spacing stays fixed — lengthening those would look wrong).

Geometry is set up with `geometry.morphTargetsRelative = true` and one
`BufferAttribute` per axis in `geometry.morphAttributes.position` (built once,
inside the same expensive `useMemo` as the base mesh in `Mannequin.tsx`).
Applying a weight is a **separate, cheap `useEffect`** that just writes numbers
into the already-allocated `mesh.morphTargetInfluences` array — changing size
or body shape never rebuilds geometry.

**Sizes (XS–XXXL)** are weight presets, not new meshes — solved from the
M-reference measurements via `resolveSizeMorphWeights()`. **Body-shape
presets** (Slim/Regular/Athletic/Curvy/Plus/Tall/Petite/Custom,
`BODY_SHAPE_WEIGHTS`) are named weight vectors across all 10 axes, summed with
the size weights via `combineMorphWeights()` (clamped to ±1.6 so extrapolation
stays plausible — "Custom" exposes the raw sliders with no preset nudge).

**Known approximation**: summing independent single-axis linear morphs is not
exact — accuracy degrades furthest from the M reference (XS, XXXL). No
acceptance-gate test compares morph-blended output against a literal
re-loft at the target measurements yet; the Vitest suite only checks
direction (XS negative, XXXL positive) and that each axis actually displaces
vertices. Revisit if extreme sizes look visibly off once real garments are
fitted against them in Phase 3.

## Collision proxy (Phase 2)

`src/components/3d/mannequin/MannequinCollision.ts` — `buildCollisionProxy()`
builds one capsule (or, for hands, one sphere) per body region, rigidly
positioned from the same bone world positions the visible mesh uses. This is
the industry-standard technique for body/cloth collision (Marvelous Designer,
CLO3D, and every game-engine ragdoll use capsule/sphere chains, not a second
detailed mesh) — deliberately not a lower-poly second loft. Radii are rough
measurement-derived approximations (not re-derived from the visible surface),
since a collision volume only needs to roughly contain the body. Stays under
~1k triangles total, well inside the 5k–20k collision budget. Rendered as a
wireframe overlay behind `Mannequin`'s `showCollisionDebug` prop (QA-only, off
by default) — not consumed by anything yet, since there's no cloth
simulation until Phase 4.

## Materials

`src/components/3d/mannequin/MannequinMaterials.ts` — `createMannequinMaterial()`
takes either a preset id (`ivory` / `beige` / `stone` / `white`, all
metalness 0, roughness 0.48–0.6) or a free-form `{color, roughness, metalness}`
config. Never hardcoded, per the brief's material-system requirement.

## Swapping in a real GLB

`src/components/3d/mannequin/MannequinLoader.ts`'s `useGltfMannequinAsset()`
is real, working code wrapping drei's `useGLTF` — nothing calls it today
because no licensed asset exists, but `Mannequin.tsx` already branches on
`MannequinSource.kind` (`"procedural"` vs `"gltf"`), so pointing a
`Mannequin.baseModelUrl` (the Prisma field, already in the schema) at a real
`.glb` file and passing `{ kind: "gltf", url }` as the `source` prop is the
entire integration — no rendering code changes required.

## What's NOT built yet (deferred phases)

Phases 1 and 2 are done — silhouette, professional appearance, correct
proportions, clean topology, size morphing, and a collision proxy (the first
six items in the brief's own 10-item priority order). Not yet done from
Phase 2's original scope: closing the `Product.gender`/`Mannequin.gender`
plumbing gap (never threaded through `ProductExperience` → `GarmentViewer`
today) — deferred to Phase 3 below, since it only matters once `GarmentViewer`
needs to pick a mesh, and doing it in isolation now would have nothing to
wire it to.

- **Phase 3**: closing the `Product.gender`/`Mannequin.gender` plumbing gap,
  real skeletal animation clips, cutting `GarmentViewer` /
  `product-experience` / `HeroMannequin` over to this engine (today only
  `HeroMannequin.tsx` uses it — the PDP viewer still uses the old primitive
  system, deliberately untouched to avoid regression risk), rebuilding
  garments as their own lofted meshes sharing the body's skeleton (Three.js
  supports multiple `SkinnedMesh` on one `Skeleton`), then deleting
  `procedural/humanoid.ts`, `procedural/garment-builder.ts`,
  `scene/MannequinFigure.tsx`, and `hooks/useAnimationController.ts`.
- **Phase 4**: cloth simulation. No physics/cloth library exists in
  `package.json` — real-time solving needs a genuine dependency decision, and
  the schema already has a `SimulationAsset`/`CINEMATIC` precomputed-sequence
  path (an offline CLO3D/Marvelous/Houdini bake) that's more tractable than
  real-time solving and is how real fashion-tech platforms actually ship this.
- **Phase 5**: performance/polish (LOD, KTX2/Draco — meaningless until real
  textured GLB assets exist), admin CRUD for `Mannequin` presets, a GLB
  upload UI, the full 14-size×gender asset matrix, remaining lighting presets
  (Luxury Studio, Dramatic) and camera behaviors (close-up, full-body,
  auto-orbit).

## Known limitations / risk notes

- **Triangle budget**: ~8–20k triangles for the whole body, not the 50–150k
  figure sometimes quoted for "visible mannequin" budgets — that number fits
  a hand-sculpted mesh with surface detail; padding a smooth analytic loft to
  that count buys nothing.
- **Side-profile arm occlusion**: from an exact 90° side camera, the near arm
  (hanging close to the torso) visually overlaps much of the torso silhouette
  — this is true of any straight-arms-down human/mannequin figure, not a
  geometry bug. The torso's actual depth/curvature (bust/waist/hip) is
  visible in the three-quarter and front/back views, and partially visible
  around the arm even in the pure side view.
- **Quality ceiling**: this reads as "clean, smooth, correctly-proportioned"
  at normal viewing distances. It will not reach hand-sculpted,
  ZBrush-retopologized fidelity (natural asymmetry, anatomical nuance) — that
  requires a real authored asset, which is exactly what the GLB loader path
  exists to accept later.
- **Where the engine is actually wired in today**: the homepage hero
  (`HeroMannequin.tsx`) and an internal admin QA route
  (`/admin/3d-studio/mannequin-preview`). The customer-facing product page's
  3D viewer (`GarmentViewer`) still runs the old primitive system — cutting
  it over is Phase 3, once garments move to the same skeleton so nothing
  visibly clips during the transition.
